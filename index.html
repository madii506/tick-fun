05 — THE RULES

How a tick drinks.

05.1

The host

Any pump.fun coin that trades against SOL or USDC, on its bonding curve or in its PumpSwap pool. A tick can't host another tick, and a mayhem-mode coin can't host. Paste an address in 01 and the site asks pump.fun's program before you spend anything.

05.2

The launch

The engine creates your coin with pump.fun's own create_v2 instruction, priced in the host. Its starting price comes from the host's live price, so it opens like any pump.fun coin, measured in the host instead of SOL.

05.3

Buying a tick

Buyers still pay in SOL. pump.fun routes it in one transaction: SOL buys the host, the host buys the tick. Selling runs the same route backwards.

05.4

The drink

A tick's bonding curve holds the host coins its buyers paid in. While people hold the tick, those host coins stay locked in its curve. When the tick graduates, they move into its PumpSwap pool. The board shows how much each tick holds, read from the chain.

05.5

The fees

pump.fun pays a tick's creator fee in the host coin, into the tick's own engine wallet. Every tick has its own wallet, so no two ticks' fees ever mix.

05.6

The split, every hour

Every hour the engine claims what is waiting. Half goes to the launcher, in the host coin. The other half is sold for SOL, buys $TICK, and that $TICK is burned.

05.7

The cost

A launch costs 0.03 SOL plus any first buy. It pays pump.fun's rent for the new coin and the transaction fees of the tick's hourly claims. Your first buy is made in the same minute and sent to your wallet.

05.8

Before $TICK exists

Until $TICK is live, the buyback half waits in each tick's engine wallet, in the host coin. The first hour after $TICK appears on this page, the engine sells it and burns what it buys.

Who runs it

The engine runs on this site's server. It holds the keys to every tick's engine wallet, derived from one secret. That makes TICK custodial for creator fees, between a claim and its payout, usually less than a minute. Nobody else can move them, and nothing else sits in those wallets.

What could go wrong, and where you'd see it

  • The engine stops claiming: the tick's page shows the last claim and the fees still waiting.
  • A payout is wrong: every payout links its transaction, next to the claim it came from.
  • The host dies: the tick keeps trading, priced in a coin nobody wants.
07 — FAQ

The obvious questions.

Why would a host's holders want a tick on their coin?

Every tick buy is a buy of the host, and every tick that people hold keeps host coins locked in its curve. A host with a hundred ticks has a hundred curves holding its supply.

Do I need the host's permission?

No. Any pump.fun coin that trades against SOL or USDC can host. Nobody behind a host is asked, contacted or paid, and a tick on a coin is not an endorsement by that coin.

What do I get as the launcher?

Half of your tick's creator fees, every hour, paid in the host coin to the wallet you launched from. Plus whatever your first buy got you.

What happens to the other half?

It is sold for SOL, the SOL buys $TICK, and the $TICK is burned. Before $TICK exists it waits in the tick's engine wallet, in the host coin.

Can a tick host another tick?

No. pump.fun allows one level: a host must itself trade against SOL or USDC.

What if the host dumps?

Your tick is priced in the host, so its value in SOL moves with the host as well as with its own buyers. A tick on a dead host is a coin priced in a dead coin.

Is TICK part of pump.fun?

No. TICK uses pump.fun's public program and SDK. Ticks are ordinary pump.fun coins: you can find them, chart them and trade them on pump.fun.

What is $TICK?

TICK's own coin. You don't need it to launch or to trade a tick. Half of every tick's fees buy it and burn it, every hour.
